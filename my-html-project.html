Ancient History

The Cradle of Civilization

Ethiopia is one of the oldest countries in the world and the only African country never to be colonized. Its history spans over 3,000 years, with the Aksumite Kingdom (c. 100-940 AD) being one of the great powers of the ancient world.

The Kingdom of Aksum was a major trading empire with connections reaching as far as India and China. It was one of the first major empires to convert to Christianity in the 4th century AD under King Ezana.

Ethiopian cuisine reflects this long history, with injera (fermented flatbread) dating back over 6,000 years. The unique flavors come from berbere (spice mixture) and niter kibbeh (spiced clarified butter), both with ancient origins.

The Legend of Queen of Sheba

According to Ethiopian tradition, the Queen of Sheba (Makeda) traveled from Ethiopia to Jerusalem to visit King Solomon. Their union resulted in a son, Menelik I, who became the first emperor of Ethiopia and brought the Ark of the Covenant to Ethiopia.

This legend forms the basis of Ethiopia's Solomonic dynasty, which ruled until 1974, claiming direct descent from King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba.

Coffee Ceremony

Buna ቡና: The Ethiopian Coffee Ceremony

The Ethiopian coffee ceremony is an integral part of Ethiopian social and cultural life. It's an elaborate ritual that can take several hours, emphasizing community, respect, and hospitality.

Ethiopia is the birthplace of coffee (Coffea arabica), and the ceremony honors this legacy. The word "coffee" comes from the Ethiopian region of Kaffa.

1
Roasting: Green coffee beans are washed and roasted in a pan over hot coals.
2
Grinding: The roasted beans are ground using a mortar and pestle.
3
Brewing: Ground coffee is added to a traditional clay pot called a jebena with water.
4
Serving: Coffee is served in small cups with plenty of sugar, often accompanied by popcorn or incense.

At Gursha Habesha, we perform the traditional coffee ceremony daily at 4 PM. Join us to experience this authentic cultural tradition.
